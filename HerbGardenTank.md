# Grow Herbs in a Rigid Stock Tank

## Filling the Tank (Bottom to Top)

1. **Wire mesh** over the drain plug so soil doesn't escape
2. **Thin gravel/rock layer** (2–3″) for drainage — keep it modest to save space
   and weight
3. **Organic filler** (cardboard, dead branches, leaves, twigs, straw) — fills
   the middle and breaks down into compost over time; this is the hugelkultur
   approach
4. **Inexpensive topsoil** to fill gaps in the filler
5. **Potting mix and compost** (roughly 50/50) for the top 8 to 12″ where roots
   actually grow — add perlite for extra drainage
6. **Mulch** (a couple of inches) on the surface to hold moisture in Austin heat

## Edible Plants for Warm Shade in Austin

| **Plant**                        | **Notes**                                                                                |
|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Mints (various)                  | Thrive in part shade, very hardy, container-friendly; keep separate or they'll take over |
| Lemon balm                       | Likes afternoon shade, easy from seed or cuttings, great for tea                         |
| Oregano                          | Nearly indestructible in Austin, survives heat and drought, reseeds freely               |
| Mexican / Cuban oregano          | Even more heat- and drought-tolerant than common oregano, native to the Southwest        |
| Basil (especially Thai/Genovese) | Grows well in partial shade; replant each spring in Austin, bolts in peak heat           |
| Parsley                          | Tolerates sun and shade well, reseeds in the yard                                        |
| Chives                           | Shallow-rooted, do fine in part shade, perennial                                         |
| Cilantro                         | Best in cool season (fall–early spring); bolts in summer heat but reseeds readily        |
| Rosemary                         | Handles part shade, set-and-forget once established                                      |

**Key tip for Austin shade:** mulch is critical — it regulates soil temperature
and conserves moisture, which is what makes shade-tolerant herbs survive the
summer heat. Water in the morning or late evening.
