# Hügelkultur

[Hügelkultur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hügelkultur) (German for "mound
culture" or "hill culture") is a centuries-old gardening technique originating
in **Eastern Europe and Germany**, popularized in modern permaculture by Sepp
Holzer. It involves building a raised garden bed from **layers of decaying wood
and organic matter**, which slowly decompose over years to create a
self-sustaining, moisture-rich growing environment.

## How it works

The core idea: instead of filling a bed entirely with expensive topsoil, you
pack the bottom 60–70% with free woody debris. As it rots, it:

1. **Holds water** like a sponge, releasing it slowly to plant roots
2. **Feeds plants** with a steady nutrient release as decomposition progresses
3. **Builds soil** over time, mimicking natural forest-floor decomposition

## Layers (bottom to top)

| Layer          | Material                                        |
|----------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Base           | Largest logs and branches                       |
| Middle         | Smaller branches, twigs, wood chips             |
| Nitrogen layer | Grass clippings, leaves, manure, kitchen scraps |
| Top            | Subsoil → 3 to 4 in. compost → 1–2 in. topsoil  |

## Key tips

- **Best woods:** cottonwood, oak, apple, maple, birch. **Avoid:** black locust
  and black walnut (allelopathic).
- **No dig required** — you can build directly on the ground, though a shallow
  trench helps anchor the structure.
- **Water each layer** as you build to kick-start decomposition.
- The bed will **settle and shrink** over the first 2–3 years as the wood breaks
  down.

## Payoff

By about **year 3**, the bed holds so much water that irrigation is often
unnecessary, and by **year 4** the soil is exceptionally fertile. It's
especially well-suited to **dry climates, poor soil, or compacted urban lots**
where traditional raised beds struggle.
